What cleaning customers look for

Hiring a cleaner means letting someone into your home, often while you're not there. That makes trust the first hurdle. Visitors look for signs that you're a real, accountable business: bonding and insurance, background checks, a satisfaction promise, clear policies on keys and pets, and the faces of your team.

The second hurdle is price. Many cleaning companies hide pricing behind a phone call, and many customers simply leave. Showing "starting at" prices by home size, or an instant price estimator, tends to filter out bad-fit leads and make good-fit customers comfortable booking.

What we build for cleaning and personal service businesses

  • Clear service menu — standard, deep, move-in/move-out, office and add-ons like windows, carpets or laundry.
  • Pricing guidance by bedrooms and bathrooms, with recurring-visit discounts called out.
  • A booking request flow that collects home size, preferred day and contact details in under a minute.
  • Trust section covering insurance, screening, supplies and your re-clean policy.
  • Works for other personal services too — pet sitting, organizing, laundry, mobile detailing — anything where the customer books a visit.

Repeat business is the real prize

A single deep clean is nice. A bi-weekly customer for three years is a business. Your website should make recurring service the obvious choice: explain the savings, the same-cleaner consistency and how easy it is to skip or reschedule. We write the copy to steer visitors that way.
