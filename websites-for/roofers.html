Why roofing sites are different

Roofing customers move slowly, then suddenly. Most of the year they're researching quietly — comparing contractors, reading about shingle types and wondering about insurance. Then a hail storm rolls through and everyone in the zip code searches at once. Your website has to serve both moments: patient, educational content for the researchers and a fast "request a free inspection" path for storm season.

Trust is the whole game. Homeowners have heard stories about storm chasers who knock on doors and disappear. A local roofer's site should make it obvious that you're established nearby: a real address or service area, licensing and insurance information, manufacturer certifications if you have them, warranty details, and lots of photos of finished roofs.

What we build for roofing contractors

  • Free inspection request form front and center, with optional photo upload so homeowners can show you the damage.
  • Storm and insurance claim page that explains, in plain language, how you work with the homeowner's insurance process.
  • Project gallery organized by roof type — asphalt, metal, flat and repairs — so visitors can find jobs like theirs.
  • Service pages for replacement, repair, gutters, siding and commercial roofing, each targeting its own searches.
  • Financing section if you offer it, since payment options are a top question on big jobs.

Getting found locally and in AI search

Roofing is a competitive local search. We set up clean page titles, fast load times, mobile-first design and structured data describing your business and service area, plus your Google Business Profile. That foundation helps both traditional search results and AI assistants that recommend local contractors.
